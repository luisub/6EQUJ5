# Existing scenes, travel and art inventory — v2

Read-only static inspection, 2026-11-11. This is a blockout proposal and inventory, not a level change, human playtime measurement, motion review or art acceptance. The current project is untracked; preserve existing sources and exports.

## What “longer sequences” can mean here

Longer **place-to-place travel** is mainly a room-size, camera, collision and encounter-layout question. Additional animation frames can improve a repeated walk cycle but do not by themselves increase distance travelled. Keep ordinary movement speed unchanged for an initial comparison. The source supports a focused trial in the approach and salvage yard; it does not establish that every scene should expand.

The field viewport is 480 × 270 world pixels. `data/balance.json` and `PlayerConfig` declare walk 140 and run 180 px/s; however, the ordinary horizontal target in `engine/player.py` uses walk speed (half when crouched), not `run_speed`. Ground acceleration/deceleration and special traversal alter instantaneous velocity. The animator selects run only above 1.1 × walk speed (154 px/s), so ordinary steady movement selects walk; special movement/impulses may select other states. The Mateo manifest defines two walk frames at 180 ms and four run frames at 110 ms for each outfit; that describes animation cycles, not a normal sprint input. Field and boss load olive-field clips with a 0.25 scale factor: a 96 × 128 source cell corresponds to a 24 × 32 logical/world cell. The accepted density-2 migration renders that footprint at 48 × 64 native output pixels; historically the 480 × 270 renderer used 24 × 32 output pixels. Visible alpha occupies less than the cell. Bunker has its own representation.

At 140 px/s and already at full speed, 480 px is 3.43 s of hypothetical uninterrupted horizontal movement. The following values divide selected **x-coordinate differences** by 140; they omit acceleration, collision, gaps, combat, interaction, jumps, backtracking, loading and stops. ### Existing encounter spacing and route constraints

`FieldOpsScene._setup_encounter` initializes the approach drone at x440 with patrol x380–500, and hound at x660 with patrol x580–760: initial x separation 220 px, patrol intervals separated by 80 px. The salvage yard reuses the drone at x440/patrol 380–500 and places its hound at x560/patrol 480–600: initial x separation 120 px, patrol intervals overlap by 20 px. These are authored initial positions and bounds, not measured in-play separations; movement, detection and collision affect the encounter. The catwalk clears ordinary room enemies and conditionally starts defense after repair. Annex/relay/flashback do not receive the ordinary approach pair; annex has separately placed optional faction encounters.

The authored route links approach → salvage; yard → catwalk is gated by feedhorn repair; catwalk returns to approach. Bunker code can send the player directly to the catwalk for defense and to boss under progression conditions. Checkpoint triggers, safe spawns, objective/repair flags, exits, scripted test drivers and persistent room-state coordinates all require review if geometry changes. Resume continues at the bunker. Do not assume a renamed or moved room will preserve existing saves or route behavior without testing.

## Proposed focused blockout experiment

1. **Approach: 960 → 1,440 × 288** (60 → 90 tiles, three viewport widths; camera x range 0–960). Keep entrance near x48 and place an exit around x1,392, subject to collision validation. This is 1,344 px of illustrative horizontal separation, or 9.60 s at steady 140 px/s: approximately 3.43 s more idealized open-ground travel than the existing x48→912 comparison. Shape three distinct beats: arrival/cover introduction, a hazard and readable encounter, then dish approach and the yard gate. Place a safe checkpoint around x720 and distribute enemies/cover with adequate warning and safe retreat; verify all placements rather than simply stretching existing intervals.
2. **Salvage: 640 → 960 × 288** (40 → 60 tiles, two viewport widths; camera x range 0–480). Test entry near x48, a safe midpoint around x480, and coupler/depot around x840. The x48→840 separation is 792 px, or 5.66 s in the same idealized model: approximately 2.29 s more than today's x48→520 comparison. Build a legible route through scrap, a separate encounter/cover decision and a recognisable depot; preserve the repair-return loop and avoid padding the return with empty space. Place an exit only after confirming it works with the objective gate and return route.
3. Keep the 640-wide catwalk and arena and 480-wide interaction/prototype rooms compact for the initial trial. Their purposes depend on defense geometry, boss patterns, interaction and information density, not a long run. Reconsider individually after normal play.

These x positions are **unvalidated blockout targets**, not spawn-safe placements or final encounter schedules. Compare existing and proposed rooms at identical speed, viewport/world scale and available controls. Use real movement and combat with fresh and continued saves; inspect every camera segment, approach directions, checkpoints, respawn, hazard readability, enemy telegraphs, cover, exit gates, objective persistence and return travel. Measure human play separately, and distinguish it from scripted or unobstructed estimates. Retain recoverable masters and originals before altering untracked files.

## Scene and asset coverage for a high-contrast pass

| Registered scene / room family | Existing rendering/assets and callers | Scope cue |
|---|---|---|
| Title | `engine/scenes/title.py` draws boot/save UI and stars procedurally. | Contrast typography, selections, recovery and errors; no horizontal route. |
| Bunker hub | `scenes/bunker.py` requests six 480 × 270 bunker layers, character strips/bunker sprite and civilization images via `engine/assets.py`; stations lead to lab, terminal, field and progression-dependent routes. | Preserve station affordances, player contact, readable focus and the foreground/light compositing order. |

| Field: approach, yard, catwalk, annex, relay, flashback | `scenes/field.py` loads room data and olive-field animation. `engine/world.py` hardcodes Ohio tileset, sky, treeline/fence, dish lattice and mist cache rather than selecting them per room; it draws some objects and effects procedurally. | Shared references mean a global replacement affects multiple rooms, including prototype interiors; room-specific art requires explicit routing and state-aware review. Maintain collision silhouettes and hostile/telegraph contrast. |
| Boss | `scenes/boss.py` loads the arena, olive-field animation and Greenkeeper; `engine/bosses/greenkeeper.py` loads 11 named boss-frame files. | Preserve boss weak points, warning cues, cover and HUD alongside environment contrast. |

The Ohio export manifest names a 256 × 128 tileset, three 960 × 270 background layers and 480 × 270 mist. `world.py` repeats background surfaces with 0.10/0.35/0.65 horizontal parallax and provides a procedural fallback if assets fail. Existing longer-room designs therefore need repetition/seam and landmark review, not merely a wider collision array. The same world renderer caches these images. Nine enemy folders are loaded by `EnemyManager`, and the Greenkeeper loader requests named frames independently; do not treat the global asset manifest as a complete inventory of all scene art.

`assets/sprites/mateo/manifest.json` lists navy, olive and Faraday outfits with 15 animation states each; field and boss callers explicitly request olive. The bunker uses separately loaded strips/sprite. The civilization manifest lists FASR-001 through FASR-010 and FASR-SCAN-H; terminal and bunker call civilization images through the asset cache. The top-level `assets/manifest.json` contains 66 entries, all marked `authored_lock: true`; static path checking found every non-null `source_path` and every listed `relative_path` present. Seven entries have no source path: FASR-SCAN-H's four image variants, palette sheet, and two music files. A null path is missing provenance in that manifest, not proof that no recoverable master exists elsewhere. Ohio's `art/source/environments/ohio/` contains a material/palette JSON; no original Ohio background or tileset master image is recorded there. Enemy/boss provenance and independent backups were not exhaustively audited here. Existing generated visual-upgrade candidates are separate review sources; do not overwrite them or infer approval.

`planning/QUALITY_GATES.md` describes 14 locked build groups (11 civilization groups plus Mateo, bunker and review); the latest current-state checkpoint reports the dry run preserved them. Locks, outputs and sources must remain intact while developing candidates. A later integrated pass needs fresh runtime captures at native and integer scale, CRT on/off, motion, visual quality and normal input; static inventory does not close the open art or G2/G3 gates.
