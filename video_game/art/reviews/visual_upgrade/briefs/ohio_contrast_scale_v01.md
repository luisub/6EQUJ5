## Composition and visual hierarchy

Use bold *light/dark contrast*: near-black cool shadow masses at outer edges frame a comparatively open, illuminated center; deep navy/violet ambient gives way to distinctly lit amber concrete and metal faces, with a few orange work lights that cast coherent, shaped shadows. Use broad near-black structural shadows against those lit faces; do not turn the scene into a uniform lavender-blue fog. The center should feel like a lived-in maintenance route rather than a generic sky behind disconnected props. Pools of light are painted on **background architecture and surfaces**, not invented walkable ground; a visible service apron behind retained terrain at approximately y=390–440 may carry a pool if it does not imply a platform or obscure action. The retained runtime floor itself cannot acquire a painted pool from a backdrop. Existing actual floor, hazards and platforms remain plainly discernible in the composite. The brightest accents belong to a few motivated light sources and actual gameplay cues, with restrained reflected light to describe form. Use substantial overlapping masses and hard shadow boundaries instead of haze. Add subdued structural supports or wall shadow masses **behind** the actual platform spans, aligned with their existing positions so they read as catwalks rather than isolated floating pieces; they must not create new climbable surfaces or conceal the true lips.

- **Far:** the monumental Big Ear reflector and trusses, substantially darker than foreground actors, broken into grouped structural masses with clear scale. A layered autumn treeline and narrow dusk sky create depth; no uniform wall of wire or bright repeated lattice. Preserve the side view and camera crop.
- **Middle left, approximately x=120–320, y=100–300:** receding brick/concrete maintenance building, broad pilasters and an inset service-bay facade; pipes, conduits and limited signage establish the scientific site's utility. Keep the actual airlock and its label clear below/left. Place one concrete utility lamp around `(150,315)` and give the background facade/apron around x=70–210, y=350–440 a shaped warm pool that separates Mateo; use a defined light source and hard shadow shapes, not a halo or filter. Light substantial faces of masonry and metal; it must not imitate an interactive marker.
- **Middle center/right, approximately x=560–900, y=100–300:** a **boxy 1980s/1990s maintenance vehicle** parked in a set-back yard plane, smaller than the foreground play scale, with simple windows, wheels and weathered steel body. It is scenery, never an implied driveable vehicle, collision, cover or usable roof. Integrate one or two distant bins, a service cabinet, pipework and a cable reel into distinct plausible clusters at varied depth, not a repeated inventory line. Make their contrast subordinate to the real player/platforms; do not place a high-contrast roofline along any real platform top.
- **Near framing:** deep shadowed structural/tree silhouettes at the outer edges can add the reference's weight. Put these in the opaque backdrop behind gameplay; no separate foreground overlay. Do not obscure enemies, projectiles, HUD or conditional prompts. No dark veil across the player, traversable lane or sump.
- **Materials:** chunky masonry, patched concrete, oxidized painted steel, rubber tires, worn cabinet enamel and copper cabling need distinct broad shapes and selected pixel clusters. Add hand-authored joints, repairs, panel seams, purposeful dirt and small accents; avoid procedural speckle, all-over crosshatching or smooth filtered gradients as a substitute for design. Make the architecture, vehicle, bins, pipes, reels and lighting feel like one coherent yard with a shared perspective, scale and light direction. The requested complexity should be visible at 960×540, not only in an oversized source.
