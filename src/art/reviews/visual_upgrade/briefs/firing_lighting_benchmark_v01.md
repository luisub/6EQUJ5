# Ohio approach — local firing-light benchmark v01

**Status: proposed for coordinated review.** One bounded visual experiment in the existing Ohio approach, using the currently installed original Mateo and actual room geometry. The larger armed 60×80 candidate is not installed. Do not regenerate the environment, create a batch of effects, change other rooms or alter any gameplay rule as part of this benchmark.

## Intent and current baseline

Make a fired maser briefly cast a convincing **local warm/pink light** onto Mateo and nearby surfaces, surrounded by the existing darker cool scene. The visual reference is the relationship of bright, shaped pools and strong shadows to cooler surroundings, adapted to this side-on scientific service yard. Keep the existing room framing and materials, navy sky, warm practical lamp, readable actor and clear traversal geometry. Avoid a flat opaque green bulb, full-screen black filter, wash of bloom, permanent glow, new wall or implied hazard. The contrast should come primarily from the localized flash and reflected light, not turning the entire screen dark.

Actual baseline: `previews/mateo_fire_preview_v02/right_original_frame0_native_960.png` and corresponding left view are current original-actor physical-J stills in the installed approach. The existing `EffectManager` draws a pale diamond and an opaque green circle with nominal **0.08-second** life; it is a graphic over the scene, not verified reflected illumination. `FieldOpsScene` currently adds the visual from the newly created projectile after the player update and draws projectiles/effects after the actor. The first shot may use the preceding idle clip's gameplay muzzle rather than the later recoil clip's attachment. Use actual event/spawn coordinates and the existing camera transform, not a fixed screen location or a newly invented weapon origin.

## One visual specification

- **Core and direction:** a small, broken, directional flash at the actual shot origin, with hot pale ivory `(255,228,202)`, a coral-pink `(255,122,154)` edge and restrained warm amber `(255,164,95)` bounce. Initial native target for the brightest core is no larger than about 12×8 pixels, with a shaped directional flare within about 24×16 pixels; its axis follows the actual shot direction. These are review targets, not collision or projectile dimensions. No solid circular green overlay, detached light source, large opaque flare or permanent mark.
- **Near surfaces:** starting envelope is an elliptical soft falloff **144×112 native pixels**, radii 72×56 at density 2 (36×28 logical world units), centered on the real shot origin and clipped to the viewport. It is a maximum local bound, not a uniformly filled disc: the hot core is small, the warm/rose reflection fades quickly through mid-values, and the outer boundary reaches zero. An initial visual target is roughly 20–30% local warm lift at the strongest receptive nearby wall/ground area; adjust only after actual material and readability review. Preserve texture, structural edges, real floor/platform cues and the existing lamp's separate steady pool. Nearby means only visible surfaces within that bound, not the whole room.
- **Mateo:** include a restrained warm edge/bounce on the muzzle-facing arm, jacket and face where the local light reaches him. Clip this response to the current sprite's alpha and preserve its material colors, facial silhouette and existing size. Start around 20–35% peak local lift, tapering across the body; do not paint a colored disc over the actor, make the whole sprite uniformly pink, alter blink or create a larger collider. If the sprite is not visible due to invulnerability flashing, do not draw a ghost sprite.
- **Timing:** tie both core and local reflected light to the existing shot effect lifetime of **0.08 s** and its actual pause behavior. Start promptly, decay smoothly to zero and remove completely; do not extend gameplay firing, cooldown, projectile lifetime or input. At 60 Hz inspect onset, intermediate samples and the first off frame, acknowledging the existing effect is updated in the same tick and sample times may differ from an idealized timeline. Repeated shots must not accumulate lingering light.

The scene already provides a dark/cool field of values. If a small cool ambient adjustment is needed for this one benchmark, apply it to the underlying approach environment only, initial ceiling **10% luminance reduction**, retaining original textures and existing practical light. It must not be a black overlay, flatten the scene, dim the actor, enemies, hostile telegraphs, projectiles, HUD or pause layer, or replace the local illumination. Compare the same no-fire scene and retain the original path as fallback; a constant global dim is not evidence of a successful shot response.

## Order and asset contract

Preserve the current field draw order and actual scene geometry. A safe approach is to apply bounded light to already-rendered environment/terrain before enemy bodies and attack telegraphs, and separately apply an alpha-clipped response to Mateo at his existing depth. Then retain actual projectile and flash visibility, weather, interaction text, HUD and pause as the current runtime requires. Exact implementation belongs to the runtime owner: do not tint a flattened final frame containing HUD/telegraphs or paint over actors, UI, signals or hazard boundaries. Account for camera movement and clipping, and ensure the light cannot leak outside its specified viewport/world bound. Boss, other rooms and other weapon types remain on their existing behavior unless a later explicitly reviewed change covers them. Preserve aim, damage, ammo, cooldown, projectile path, physics, camera, actor size, attachments, hitboxes, collisions and saves.

The local falloff may be a bounded mathematical light mask; no new authored bitmap is required to test the mechanism. If an authored effect sprite becomes necessary, commission **one** separate 144×112 RGBA transparent master/export with source point `(72,56)`, no opaque background or HUD, and preserve its full generated master, actual dimensions, prompt and hashes. A proportional reviewed export and isolated alpha-edge checks are required; a generated master may not arrive at exact requested dimensions. Do not claim a procedural mask is an artist-authored sprite or use it as a substitute for an approved bitmap.

Show actual onset/decay/off samples and a short time-ordered playback if available; include a second rapid shot, camera-edge clipping, pause/resume, respawn/scene exit, existing telegraph overlap and CRT on/off.
