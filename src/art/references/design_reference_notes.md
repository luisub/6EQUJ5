# Phase 1 visual references — v1

Research date: 2026-11-11. Purpose: inform the Ohio approach and Mateo olive-field resolution comparison; principles are proposals, not an approved visual standard or a claim of in-engine acceptance. Borrow visual relationships, not reference characters, architecture, logos, exact palettes, or compositions.

Its linked full-size gameplay JPEGs returned fetch errors in this session. The [Huntdown gameplay preview in Vooks](https://www.vooks.net/huntdown-switch-review/) was available as an image-search preview: it shows a dark industrial scene with crates and two levels occupied by small figures and bright gunfire. This is a secondary image and preview-scale observation; do not infer original pixel dimensions, animation, or actual gameplay readability from it.
- [Lou Bagel's comparison](https://www.loubagel.com/blog/beautiful-pixel-art-games/) supplies gameplay screenshots of Kingdom: Classic, Huntdown and Star Renegades; the author says the examples are geographically close to each other and do not show the games' full range. The author describes Kingdom's changing sky and water reflections, and notes that Star Renegades mixes pixel art with non-pixel effects. These are the author's observations, not a technical breakdown of either renderer.

## Transferable direction

| Reference cue | Interpretation to test in Ohio | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Huntdown: compact figures and cover in a layered industrial action scene, with bright transient gunfire. | Make cover edges, walkable tops and silhouettes legible first. Describe steel with a few grouped edge/highlight/shadow shapes; use rust at seams and exposed edges rather than uniform speckle. Give concrete broader, lower-contrast chips; use short directional highlights for wet metal. | Decorative seams must not resemble collision edges. Reserve the strongest warm/cool accents for actionable cues, enemies and fire. Keep Ohio's observatory and Rust Belt identity instead of reproducing Huntdown's city or props. |
| Kingdom: dark massing with luminous accents and reflected light. | Separate sky, distant dish lattice/treeline and the playable trench with distinct value bands; simplify distant geometry and keep it less contrasty than the play plane. Let a few pools of light anchor depth. | No water reflection is required where no water exists. Mist, foreground and parallax must not conceal a hound, projectile or platform lip. |
| Star Renegades: localized saturated light against darker environments. | Test a restrained cold ambient field with warm work-lamp accents, and a small localized cyan/green effect on equipment when motivated by a source. Let larger color shapes describe volume before isolated bright pixels. | The turn-based reference is not proof that equivalent effects work during fast action. Do not flood the whole frame with glow; preserve telegraph hue/value separation and legible HUD. |
| Inspected Mateo 48 × 64 asset: small dark olive/khaki figure with light face and scattered coat highlights. | At gameplay scale, evaluate head, shoulders, arm/weapon, coat hem and separate legs as coherent clusters. Preserve the olive coat/khaki trouser identity and foot contact; use a selective rim or local background contrast only where the silhouette merges. | Do not add face/detail pixels merely because a larger canvas permits them. Check idle, run, jump, crouch, slide, shoot and cover at matching on-screen size, not only an enlarged still. Preserve pivots and weapon attachment in any later implementation. |

## Resolution comparison protocol

Use identical camera framing, apparent Mateo height, scene content, weather, effects and display size for each candidate; disclose source pixel dimensions, sprite cell and visible alpha bounds, and integer scaling/filtering. The 960 × 540 high-density candidate uses a 48 × 64 cell at twice the current rendering density, keeping the same world-relative and displayed character size; it does not make Mateo or the world larger. Compare native and integer-scaled views, plus a motion/contact sheet. Judge silhouette and pose recognition; platform and cover collision truth; projectile and telegraph visibility against every layer; material distinction; and the amount of visually idle space around combat. Include CRT on/off if applicable. A larger source or more colors is not automatically better: choose the smallest candidate that meets the readability and material goals when viewed in context. Inspect real gameplay later; a static comparison cannot establish animation quality, encounter fairness, performance, or acceptance.
