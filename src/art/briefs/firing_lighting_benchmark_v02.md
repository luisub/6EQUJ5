# Ohio approach — one authored firing flash and local light v02

**Status: proposed for Astra and parent review before the single artist request and runtime experiment.** This revision supersedes v01's optional-bitmap framing and palette: the benchmark uses one authored flash, a separate mathematical light field, the existing original Mateo and actual room geometry. It does not install the larger pose or change other rooms, weapons or gameplay.

## Scene and light intent

Create a brief, directional maser discharge that actually appears to illuminate Mateo and nearby masonry or ground: a compact pale core, a restrained green maser edge, and a shaped warm peach/coral-pink reflection against the existing cool shadows. The goal is the strong light/dark relationship of the user's reference, translated into the actual side-on Ohio service yard. Do not replace the effect with a flat opaque green bulb, oversized bloom or a global black filter. Preserve the installed ambient image on no-fire frames; the technical owner proposes no initial ambient dimming.

The installed `assets/environments/ohio_approach_v2/world_1920x576.png` is a full-room, density-2 world layer. **Visual estimates, not authored gameplay anchors:** its two actual amber practical lamp centers are approximately source `(257,389)` and `(1082,441)`, or world `(128.5,194.5)` and `(541,220.5)`; their approximate wall pools are source x=170–407/y=410–570 and x=998–1210/y=450–570. Retain both lamps, their existing textured masonry pools and shadow shapes as the camera moves across world x=0–960. They are not fixed screen lights, new objects or collision. Camera x travels 0–480; y reaches 0–18. Do not dim, paint over or relocate these baked sources to manufacture contrast.

The baseline `previews/mateo_fire_preview_v02/right_original_frame0_native_960.png` shows the existing pale diamond and opaque green circle. `EffectManager` gives the flash nominal life **0.08 s**, updated in the firing tick; `FieldOpsScene` currently creates it from the newly created projectile after player update. The actual first shot may spawn from the preceding idle animation's muzzle. Use the actual shot spawn world position and camera transform; do not move the projectile or switch to a guessed recoil-frame origin.

## One authored flash source

Request **one** original transparent directional static RGBA flash master; intended native export **64×32**. Right-facing emitter pivot is native `(12,16)`; horizontal pixel-index mirroring maps pivot to `(51,16)`. The art extends forward to the right, remains compact around the source, and has a tightly clustered hot near-white green-tinged core, e.g. `(232,255,234)`, with a restrained `(140,215,162)` green edge. The visible core should be around 12×8 native pixels, with the principal shaped burst initially within roughly 24×16; leave transparent padding inside the 64×32 cell. Preserve a legible weapon and beam; avoid a disc, huge spike, painted environmental pool, text, actor, scenery or an opaque matte. This authored asset is the single flash frame; its runtime alpha/life envelope creates the brief pulse. No multi-frame sheet or batch.

Keep master, exact prompt, image references, actual dimensions/mode and SHA-256 in a new versioned candidate family. A generator may supply a larger image; review the master first, then derive an exact 64×32 transparent export through a disclosed proportional crop/resample, preserving the emitter registration and all relevant alpha. Inspect light and dark mattes and mirrored view. No procedural drawing may be represented as the authored sprite. Existing master and legacy effects remain recoverable. Do not show both the new burst and the old opaque green bulb for the same benchmark tap shot.

## Dynamic illumination and order

At the same real world-space shot origin, start with a finite **144×112 native-pixel** light envelope, radius `(72,56)` at 960 density = `(36,28)` logical world units. It is a falloff, not a filled ellipse: localized warm ivory/peach with a restrained coral-pink component, decreasing to zero at the boundary. Initial review targets: at peak roughly 20–30% local warm lift on receptive nearby material, and 20–35% localized actor-face/arm/jacket highlight, preserving texture and silhouette. Light must visibly hit both Mateo and nearby actual surfaces, not simply draw a translucent halo in empty air. Clip to current actor alpha or use another demonstrably equivalent method to preserve material and blink; no actor-sized solid overlay or ghost during invulnerability blink. The mathematical mask is an explicit renderer effect, not an authored bitmap. Adjust only based on registered visual evidence.

For the experiment, retain actual field sequencing through world, actor, projectile and non-muzzle effects, and weather. Composite the bounded light response, then the approved authored bright burst, **before** interaction text, HUD and pause. Preserve attack lines, enemies, projectile colors, actual platform/hazard outlines and prompt legibility; if lighting after these elements would obscure a telegraph, exclude or correctly restore it rather than bleaching it. No final-screen darkening and no changes to gameplay geometry. Use the current flash lifecycle, so pulse and reflection begin from the same actual event, decay to zero within its nominal 0.08 s and freeze on pause; the existing same-tick update determines observed frames. Repeated shots cannot accumulate residual light. Screen edges, moving camera and mirrored direction must remain registered. The benchmark is normal starter-maser taps in Ohio approach; unreviewed aim/charge/other-room behavior retains a safe existing path.
