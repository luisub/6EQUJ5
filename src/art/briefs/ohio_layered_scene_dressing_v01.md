# Ohio approach — layered scene dressing plan v01

**Planning only.** The v03 background is an accepted *depth direction study*, not a rich completed room, moving parallax asset, or production art. This plan addresses the request for more varied, lived-in detail and slow distant movement as Mateo passes nearby objects. No new art batch, generator call, runtime or collision change is authorized by this document.

## Intended scene

An aging observatory service perimeter with plausible late-1980s/1990s maintenance remnants: an old utility vehicle or service-car silhouette, lidded waste bins, electrical cabinets, practical signage, cable reels and a few weathered fixtures. Their placement and scale should vary; use broad material masses and local stories rather than repeating a prop on every tile. These are period remnants in the current setting, not a claim that this room is set in the 1990s. Keep the radio reflector and Ohio treeline legible and retain restrained dusk lighting. A vehicle must be visibly in a service area or distant plane; do not squeeze a full car into the narrow walking corridor or make its roof appear to be a usable platform.

| Plane | Proposed character and motion | Dependencies before implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Far | Dusk, distant radio structure, tree silhouettes; slowest apparent movement as camera travels. | Separately authored full-travel plate or seam-safe repeat; defined origin, coverage and alpha; camera-end and wrap review. A static cropped v03 plate proves none of these. |
| Middle | Fence, utility-yard silhouettes and an occasional period maintenance vehicle set back beyond play. Slower than world. | Separate authored mid-layer, registered to far without fake collision edges; density, parallax, wrapping, vertical coverage and lighting review. |
| World-fixed, behind actors | A few decorative bins, cabinets and specific service debris anchored to world coordinates and passed at camera speed. They do not block, protect, activate or alter physics. | New discrete RGBA prop, explicit pivot, footprint and draw order; room placement metadata/hook and tests; verify visibility against actors/projectiles and all camera positions. Current `Room.render_objects` has type-specific implementations, not a general art-driven decoration contract. |
| Foreground | Sparse edge framing below/at the screen margins, if it survives action review. | Separate transparent layer and explicit depth/occlusion rules; never mask feet, players, enemies, shots, telegraphs, exits or prompts. Omit if unreadable. |

**Decoration is not cover.** A decorative car, bin or cabinet must not appear to protect the player or block a shot. Any prop intended as collision, low/background cover, interaction or hazard requires separate gameplay/design approval, authored collision/protection/interaction definitions, test coverage and clear player-facing cues; appearance alone grants none. Never change the existing geometry to fit decoration.

## Current technical evidence and constraints

`Room.render_background` currently loads sky/fence/dish assets with hardcoded horizontal/vertical ratios **0.10/0.05**, **0.35/0.15**, **0.65/0.25** respectively, matching the environment asset manifest. It repeats 960×270 logical background plates horizontally. `approach.json` separately declares 0.15/0.10, 0.45/0.25 and 0.85/0.60; those values differ and are not the observed loaded-asset behavior. Mist uses horizontal 0.85 and an animation-time term, but normal field rendering currently passes the default time zero. World tiles, existing objects and actors use camera world-to-screen transforms, i.e. move at world speed; the camera rounds to logical pixels. A future proposal must specify the hook and measured behavior instead of assuming room metadata controls the renderer. The v03 single plate has no demonstrated separable parallax, wrap, room-wide coverage or motion.

The room is **960×288 world units**, with a **480×270** logical viewport and a **960×540** render surface. Camera x can range 0–480 and y 0–18. Settled baseline is camera `(0,18)`, foot `(56,240)` world, at screen `(56,222)` logical; top HUD occupies y=0–16, bottom y=254–269, with conditional status, prompts and combat cues elsewhere. Account for screen-fixed HUD at *all* camera positions, not only spawn. The terrain uses 16-unit tiles. The original view exposes: airlock exit `(16,176,32,48)` and terminal `(32,192,16,32)`; recess cover `(160,176,32,48)`; warning sign `(310,208,16,16)`; sump x=320–384 at y=256; low cover `(384,192,32,32)`; one-way platform tops x=160–272/y=192 and x=304–400/y=208, plus x=448–592/y=176 across the crop edge. Ground top y=240 is interrupted by the sump. The later training barrier `(512,192,16,32)`, checkpoint `(480,192)`, mast `(576,80,48,96)` and yard exit `(912,192,32,48)` are outside or partly outside the initial crop. Check actual `approach.json` and live rendering before placing anything. Drone patrol x=380–500 and hound patrol x=580–760 also require dynamic readability review; these are not safe permanent empty zones.

## One next prop to prototype after the current pose review

A single **small dented, closed steel maintenance waste bin**, muted blue-gray with a restrained rim and no luminous warning markings, placed **behind actors** near world `(240,224)` with a provisional maximum footprint **12×16 world units**, base at y=240. It is a visual-only object, not collision, low cover, an interaction, pickup, hazard or new platform. It sits below the existing overhead platform (top y=192, x=160–272), away from the airlock, recess and sump. Treat this as a *proposed anchor*, not an approved placement: test player crossing, jump/crouch/shoot, NPC/projectile silhouette, actual platform underside, camera motion and prompt/HUD overlap before approval. If it reads as usable cover or obscures action, revise or move it; do not silently add protection. No car or other props are commissioned in this step.

Deliver a future single clean native-density RGBA master with recorded actual dimensions, pivot/base, source provenance and light/dark alpha review, plus a registered fixture composite and a moving-camera review. Decide implementation placement and 2× asset sizing only after measuring world footprint, draw hook and camera behavior. Test entry, midpoint and right-clamped camera, partial-screen edges, no visual collision ambiguity, depth/occlusion, telegraph readability, no seam pop and no movement jitter. A stationary composite is a first check, not proof of parallax, gameplay fairness or acceptance. Record exact commands, fixtures, reviewer observations and untested limits. No benchmark score or release gate follows automatically.
