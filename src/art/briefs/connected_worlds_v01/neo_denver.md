# Neo Denver: first connected-world plate

Produce one original, opaque, side-on nighttime city/campus backdrop, 1920×540 native pixels, 480×270 viewport at density 2. The intended floor is world y=240, native y=480. The image pans slowly and bounded across two viewport widths. The top 55 native pixels and bottom 60 native pixels should stay quiet for HUD and traversal. Buildings are background only; collision, actor, gameplay props and UI remain separate.

The invented 2029 blackout setting uses a downtown-to-campus progression: narrow commercial brick bays, a broad dark research block with a stepped landmark, older parked wagons and vans, bins, cables and a few distant civilian figures. Keep deep navy and blue-steel shadows with isolated amber and cyan practicals. Avoid recognizable logos, phototracing, isometric stairs and fictionalizing real campus history as fact. The existing Auraria research packet describes historic brick and 1970s blocks; the fictional placement, blackout and architecture are the game's invention. Fitzsimons references inform stepped massing, not a reconstruction.

V01 retained strong varied brickwork but cropped tall skylines into the HUD zone and illuminated distant towers too strongly for the blackout. V02 revised both issues. V02 has clear dark upper and lower bands and an approximately y=480 pavement line. Planters, bollards and parked cars at actor height may read as collision or cover and must be judged in the real scene. Tiny generated pixel texture and the noninteger nearest downsample can produce uneven clusters. There is no actual gameplay, CRT, scrolling or human acceptance evidence yet. Root must decide the native export before promotion; this direction is not a complete room or art-gate acceptance.
