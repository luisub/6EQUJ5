# World expansion research v01

Research packet for art direction and future blockout, 2026-11-12. This is reference and a proposal, not approved art, a runtime change, a claim of later-act readiness, or evidence of playability. Room inventory comes from `planning/CONTENT_PLAN.md`; current behavior comes from `planning/CURRENT_STATE.md` and the room JSON. P01a remains planned pending S11. Preserve the approved characters, story, existing masters and route. Suggested civilians are background roles, not new named cast or dialogue. Photographic sources and rights are recorded in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md); use them as reference, not as cleared production assets. Visual observations below are limited to the identified images actually inspected; site pages and text supplement them. Do not infer game render resolution from reference images.

## I. Ohio — Big Ear / Perkins / Ohio Wesleyan

**Reference.** The inspected historical Big Ear aerial shows a long, curved reflector and a separate flat reflector facing across a broad rectangular ground plane, backed by trees and fields. This is a Kraus-type radio telescope, not a circular steerable dish. Big Ear was demolished in 1998; the surviving 2029 complex and Core occupation are game fiction. The inspected OWU Perkins exterior shows a low masonry building with white dome and arched openings set in a lawn among trees. The inspected Elliott Hall photo shows a pale, symmetrical multistory facade, white columns and a planted campus setting. Ohio Wesleyan's main campus and Perkins/Big Ear are separate locations; compressing or connecting them on foot would be an explicit fictional spatial adaptation, not a reconstruction.

**Art direction.** Use muted ochre/red brick, pale stone trim, old white-painted wood, tarnished steel, mesh, cable conduits, gravel, service fencing and pine silhouettes. Tell an observatory story with catalog labels, hand carts, crates, rolled cable, dustbins, utility meters, leaf litter, signs, maintenance doors and ordinary parked campus vans or older sedans. A distant optical dome can distinguish Perkins-inspired architecture from the radio array. Retain legible walking lanes and telegraphs; use small amber work lamps against cool twilight, with restrained fictional Core cyan/magenta spill. Distant groundskeepers, student visitors, archivists or maintenance staff are optional noninteractive silhouettes placed outside combat lanes; their presence in 2029 is imagined.

## II. Neo-Denver — downtown / Auraria

**Reference.** The inspected Tivoli dusk photo shows a warm-lit, arched red-brick facade and tower, framed by campus walks; the inspected campus photo shows plaza seating, paved paths and trees. Auraria's own architecture survey identifies historic brick and gables beside broad 1970s dark-brick/dark-glass blocks and later glazed buildings. Ninth Street's surviving homes represent a displaced neighborhood; their history should inform the setting rather than treat them as generic ruins. Denver DOTI describes the downtown–Auraria connection with wider sidewalks, trees, planters, seating and a protected bikeway.

**Art direction.** Build a layered street–campus transition: brick commercial bays, tall narrow windows and cornices; a broad dark-window campus block; a distant glass tower; plaza edges and a bridge/creek corridor. Use bus stops, bicycle racks, crosswalks, bins, delivery carts, newspaper scraps, bicycles, parked sedans, service vans and a transit bus as mundane storytelling. Students, campus workers, transit riders and delivery workers can appear as distant background figures. Mix existing practical shopfront and street lighting with selective fictional 2029 blackout neon, failed signs and Core infrastructure. Color and decay are game interpretation, not claims about the current campus.

## III. Area 51 — public history and desert perimeter

**Reference.** CIA's public history identifies Groom Lake as a dry lakebed used for U-2 testing and shows historical vehicles, aircraft and trailers. The inspected archival U-2 photo shows a bright, open playa, support vehicles, crew, ladder and distant mountain ridge; it is historical and does not establish today's operational layout. BLM's Nevada public-lands material supports open desert roads and distant ranges. No current restricted-site layout is inferred.

**Art direction.** Start with pale playa, windblown dust, scrub at the margins, wide empty asphalt, mountains and a sparse service approach. Suggest a perimeter through generic chain-link, warning placards, gates, guard shelter and poles, without mapping a real security arrangement. Add a fictional utility pickup, ground-service cart, road barriers, water drums and bins; distant maintenance or transport crews may provide scale. Harsh low sun, long shadows and isolated amber floodlights support legibility. S-4, the telepod vault, containment failure, Core lighting and all interior plans are explicit game fiction; invent no claim about a classified real-world interior.

## IV. VLA / Dodleston — two distinct places linked by fiction

**Reference.** The inspected NSF photograph shows a white antenna carried across a paved road on a large specialist transporter, workers guiding it and roadside vehicles; the NRAO archive identifies the track-moving operation. The inspected 1999 Historic England photo of Dodleston's Post Office/Holly Cottage shows low gabled brick cottages with chimneys and windows; the listed-building description records slate roofs and half dormers. New Mexico and Cheshire are separate real places: their rift/relay connection and temporal mechanics belong to the game.

**Art direction.** For the VLA use immense white parabolic antennas with open support structure, a specialized transporter, double rails and ties, switch hardware, dry grass, utility cabinets, maintenance carts and very distant mountains. Small technicians, track crew or visiting astronomers can establish scale. For Dodleston use compact one-and-a-half-storey brick cottages, slate gables, chimneys, modest windows, hedges, lane, post-box, bins and parked everyday cars; a plausible period computer and paper can cue the game's 1985 relay without asserting the reported messages as verified physical events. Ground each era with warm window light and cool night shadows; only the rift, timeline changes and anomalous lighting are invented.

## V. Göbekli Tepe — archaeological landscape

**Reference.** UNESCO's nomination photograph inspected on its cover shows the archaeological site in a dry limestone landscape; the inspected pillar photograph shows T-shaped monoliths and excavation context. UNESCO describes round/oval and rectangular monumental buildings and animal reliefs; the Turkish cultural ministry describes a covered excavation with a visitor walking route. Avoid presenting the site's meaning, builders or unexcavated structures as settled beyond the evidence.

**Art direction.** Use pale weathered limestone, low irregular stone walls, restrained animal relief details, tan soil and the surrounding plateau. Reference the modern shelter and visitor boardwalk as conservation infrastructure, not ancient architecture. Background roles might include archaeologists, conservators, guides and visitors; small site signs, shade structures, storage crates, brushes and a service vehicle suggest a working heritage site. Sun-baked earth and cool shelter shadow can give way to fictional Core light deeper in the imagined archive. The planetary waveguide, activated pillars and buried Core Prime arena are speculative game fiction; keep real archaeological forms recognizable and human-made.
