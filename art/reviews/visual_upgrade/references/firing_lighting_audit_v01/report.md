# Firing light and source/ordering audit — v01

2026-11-12. Read-only source audit for one installed approach benchmark. No runtime, assets, tests or saves were changed. Existing user direction calls for dark cool surrounding values, selective warm local illumination and restrained pink accents while keeping actors readable. This is a visual direction, not evidence that the current scene already behaves as a physically lit environment.

## Current firing pipeline

1. Field key events and the input mapper provide fire press/hold/release. In `FieldOpsScene.update`, `Player.update` advances movement, weapons, then the animator. The starter maser derives its projectile origin from the *current* animation clip's muzzle attachment before triggering recoil; only afterward is the animator switched to the new animation state. `BossScene.update` calls the same `Player.update` pattern.
2. Both scenes compare projectile list length before/after the player update and call `EffectManager.add_muzzle_flash` using the new last projectile's **spawn position**, facing, and charged flag. This is before projectile update moves it. The light is fixed at that world position; it does not follow the subsequent recoil pose, moving player, or moving projectile. For scattergun, the last projectile is one pellet but all pellets share the same origin.
3. `effects.py` stores radius 16 logical (normal) or 26 (charged), life/max life 0.08 s, and paints a pale flat directional diamond followed by an opaque green `(51,255,102)` circle, or amber `(255,158,44)` circle for charged. Radius and color do not decay with life. This is a painted shape, not a translucent light cast onto surrounding materials.
4. Effects update **after** being created in that same simulation update. At exact 1/60 s, birth-frame remaining life is about 0.0633 s and the four subsequent displayed states may cover roughly four frames total before removal; a birth update of 0.08 s or longer can consume the entire flash before it is rendered. Pause returns before scene update; `EffectManager.update` also explicitly freezes when paused. List capped at 16 bursts, with oldest discarded. `on_enter`/`on_exit` clear field effects; boss exit and retry clear them. `FieldOpsScene.load_room` itself does not call `effects.clear`, so direct in-scene room changes need explicit testing rather than an assumption that all old effects are purged.
5. In the field, native/legacy background → actual tiles/objects → enemies/telegraphs → native-density Mateo → projectile → effects → weather → HUD/prompts. In the boss scene, boss/telegraphs → projectiles → Mateo → effects → weather → HUD. The differing projectile/Mateo order is intentional existing behavior. Effects draw on 480 × 270 logical `crt.canvas`; native Mateo/background are flushed/composited at density 2. An additive operation must respect these staging/flush boundaries and alpha; painting after the HUD would damage readability.

## Muzzle anchor and existing fixture

`assets/sprites/mateo/manifest.json` uses olive-field source cell 96 × 128, foot pivot (48,124) and scale 0.25 logical. Idle muzzle (108,44) is foot + (±15,−20) logical; recoil muzzle (104,34) is foot + (±14,−22.5), with x sign reversed when facing left. On a grounded idle first shot, the world-fixed flash is thus 1 logical pixel farther forward and 2.5 pixels lower than the recoil attachment for the same foot position; camera rounding and movement can change the displayed displacement. Projectile origin, collision, firing direction and animation state should not be moved to conceal a presentation mismatch. Other states have their own attachment points; crouching, jumping, sliding and movement can take precedence over recoil.

The existing temporary-save physical-key fixture `previews/mateo_fire_preview_v02/context.json` records idle → recoil in both facings, exact projectile spawn at the preceding idle muzzle, and two sampled updates. The actual original still `right_original_frame0_native_960.png` shows the green circular core overlapping the pale diamond; the projectile has advanced by the first rendered sample. That fixture directly places the player and clears enemies; it is not continuous playback or proof of actual gameplay fairness. The larger candidate seen in that fixture is a one-pose research image, not installed runtime art.

## Safe benchmark boundaries

- Tie the visual event to an actual successful projectile spawn, not a raw key press. Pressing during cooldown, forbidden background cover, death, pause or the 1977 flashback must not invent a firing light. Preserve charged release timing, damage, cooldown, aim, projectile ID/origin/speed and status logic. Starter maser emits an immediate tap and a charged shot on qualifying release; scattergun emits several same-origin pellets, EMP emits one, and coilgun emits on release with `is_charged` only at later stages. The current `is_charged` classification is not a weapon identity.
- A small, short-lived warm field around the recorded world-space emitter can illuminate local background masonry/ground before enemy/actor/telegraph/HUD drawing; restrained actor-facing highlights require their own silhouette-aware composite. Design a visibly bounded falloff rather than merely replacing one opaque green circle with a larger one. Deep navy/purple shadows with selective amber light and limited pink accents are reference principles, not a request to copy architecture or brighten every surface. If the approach background is darkened for this one benchmark, retain architectural detail and contrast; prove the firing pool adds a distinct local effect over that baseline.
- Preserve the existing native world and actor detail: use an appropriately bounded surface and correct compositor flushing at native density if new light detail needs 2× resolution. Avoid changing global CRT tone, every room, source files, existing scene geometry, collision, actor size or HUD. Rendering the light underneath enemy telegraphs and actor ensures a large warm pool cannot erase them; verify any actor rim separately so it does not conceal weapon/outline/projectile.
- Place the light from the observed shot origin or document any visual-only correction against the first-shot idle/recoil difference. Check both facings, camera fractional/edge positions, near geometry, crouched/airborne state and overlap with actual actor, projectile, pickups and alerts. No coordinate change to the simulation should be required.

## Focused evidence and invariants for technical owner

1. Use actual event-driven press, held/release and updates with temporary saves and explicit fixture placement. Capture baseline/no flash, first discharge, multiple later frames and expiry at native 960 × 540 and integer-scaled review; include both facings, moving camera and at least one variable timestep/long update. Report exact dt, life and anchor coordinates. Static samples and a constructed candidate are not continuous motion acceptance.
2. Compare flash-active against matched flash-inactive frames with unchanged camera, UI and deterministic particles; local image differences should stay within documented clipped light bounds, and the extinguished image should return to baseline. Verify subtle **material** illumination appears around the emitter rather than an opaque halo. Explicitly verify no draw outside viewport, no halo on HUD/prompt text, no obscured projectile/telegraph/actor, and no altered shot-free frame. Ensure at least one native pixel check if a native-density path is claimed.
3. Assert spawn count, world origin, direction, damage, cooldown and collision results unchanged; charged vs tap discharge, scattergun multi-pellet event, EMP, coilgun release, directional aim, and no-shot cases. Confirm pause freeze, first shot after idle, repeated firing, death/retry and room exit/reentry. The current manager is capped, so stress-test bounded effects rather than allowing unbounded per-shot allocations.
4. Confirm genuine state changes and order, not just a shader buffer: background/geometry responds locally while alerts, actors and interface remain legible and their existing order holds. Compare CRT on/off and preserve world fallback behavior. Isolated headless tests are useful but native-window timing and human perception remain open.
