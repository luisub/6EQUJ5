## Silhouette and static pose

A low side-view quadruped facing left: long pointed muzzle, two erect triangular ears, compact chest, four separately jointed short legs and a long, broad, tapered wedge tail extending right. The tail is the dominant horizontal shape, distinct from the upright winged Vulture and a curled-tail Scorpion. One planted representative pose: forefeet and hindfeet on the same baseline, alert head angled slightly down; tail begins a visible lift without becoming an attack frame. Retain a clear underbelly opening between front and rear legs, a notch between ears, separation of muzzle from chest, and a visible gap above the tail root. Show a small amber/cyan warning eye; avoid a glowing full body, biological fur or a pillar silhouette.

Use the actual field Mateo idle for scale: 48×64 native cell, 25×59 visible alpha >127, native foot pivot (24,62). Proposed Fox static study: **192×128 native RGBA cell**, visible nose-to-tail length **about 100–120 px**, height **about 42–50 px** including ears and raised tail; these are art targets, not world or collision dimensions. Target ground baseline y=116 and nominal pivot `(96,116)` in the cell; anchor the support feet on y=116, preserve margin around the full silhouette, and report actual bounds/pivot after inspection. A representative view must fit without cropping and show the same baseline as Mateo. No ground line or shadow baked into transparent art.

## Materials, action and fairness

Use Vulture v02's family language without copying its design: warm pale limestone-like articulated plates as the main light value; deep umber gaps and structural limbs as the dark; aged bronze hinges as a modest middle-value accent; a small, high-contrast amber/cyan eye as the most saturated cue. Coarse deliberate pixel clusters, chipped but controlled edges, no noise or soft gradients; separate the pale animal silhouette from both pale stone and dark backgrounds. Avoid historical relief contours, decoration and inscriptions.

Proposed archive encounter only: once onscreen and facing the player, the Fox pauses, raises its tail and brightens its eye before a short committed pounce along a clearly visible floor lane. An ordinary shot during the readable windup should easily interrupt; moving or jumping clear of the marked lane is another existing-control counter. After landing it holds a long, visibly vulnerable recovery before another action. No sudden offscreen attack, extra key, invisible reach or required new reward. Exact cue, safe distance, timing, collision, spacing, access to evidence and difficulty remain for blockout and normal-input fairness tests. One pose cannot demonstrate them.
