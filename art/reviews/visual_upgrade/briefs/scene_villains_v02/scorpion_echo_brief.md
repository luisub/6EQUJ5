## Silhouette and static pose

A grounded side-view construct facing left: squat armored oval body carried on clearly separated jointed legs, two asymmetric open pincers forward and one segmented tail arching up and forward over the rear, with a blunt readable warning tip. One representative planted pose, not an attack sheet: pincers parted, tail curled high but tip held back from striking. Distinguish the silhouette from Fox's long low wedge tail and Vulture's raised wings: no pointed ears, snout or feathery forms. Keep open negative space between pincers, beneath the body between fore and hind supports, and inside the curved tail; tail tip must not merge into the body or pincers. Keep legs grouped into simple readable shapes instead of a busy tangle, with support feet sharing a baseline. A small warning lens sits at the base of the tail tip, not as an overwhelming glowing halo.

Current field Mateo idle reference is a **48×64 native cell**, with **25×59 visible** alpha >127 and foot pivot `(24,62)`. Proposed study: **192×128 native RGBA cell**, visible pincers-to-rear length **about 110–125 px**, full height **about 58–70 px including tail**. Include tail in the length measurement if it extends farther than the rear body. Ground baseline y=116, nominal registration pivot `(96,116)`, with support feet on y=116 and ample transparent margins; report actual bounds and pivot after inspection. These are art study dimensions, not collider, world, reach or room-fit decisions. Compare on the same ground line with actual Mateo.

## Materials, action and fairness

Use the accepted Vulture v02 family language while maintaining a different profile: light weathered limestone-like carapace plates dominate the body; dark umber seams and articulated legs provide separation; aged bronze forms hinges and small claw joints; restrained amber/cyan marks the warning lens. Strong light/mid/dark shapes and coarse controlled pixel clusters must read on both dark and light fields. Do not copy an archaeological carving, texture, relief composition or iconography, and do not add fur, gore or photorealism.

Proposed waveguide lane behavior only: if the guardian is fully onscreen and has a clear approach, it plants its pincers, raises its tail tip and displays a conspicuous, bounded ground marker before a short straight tail strike. A normal shot should easily interrupt the windup; moving or jumping out of the marked lane is another existing-control counter. The tail remains lowered in a long exposed recovery before resetting. Do not surprise from offscreen, cover an exit or evidence interaction, add keys, extend invisible hitboxes or stack extra threats. Final lane, timing, visual/collision agreement and counter fairness require planned blockout, animation and normal-input tests; the single still proves none of them.
